## Comparison Notes

- **The four `keryx security check-output` probes were the highest-value forty
  seconds of the run.** They turned area A1 from "call the scanner" into a design
  with two named traps — a zero exit code on `block`, and a silently downgraded
  `--target`. Neither is discoverable by reading the help text, and both would
  have produced a control that appears to work and does nothing.
- **Checking before writing removed work rather than adding it.** Two of A5's
  four perimeter rules were already implemented in helyx
  (`bot/access.ts:19`, `bot/text-handler.ts:178-184`). Three documents had to be
  corrected mid-run, and the area came out smaller and more accurate than the
  survey that preceded it had assumed.
- **The preceding keryx survey carried most of the context.** This run started
  with the five areas already identified, so Phase 1 spent its effort on the
  helyx side — citations, `file:line`, and what already exists — rather than on
  rediscovering the source material.
- **`keryx ctx rg` was the search layer throughout**, per the project rule. Its
  file-list mode was the cheapest way to decide what to open; one flag was
  rejected by its allowlist, which cost one retry.
- **The inline review caught a real contradiction**: the README claimed two areas
  were `code`-graded in keryx while the PRD's grade table said three. That is
  exactly the class of finding the review step exists for.
